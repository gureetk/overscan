| D42 | 2026-11 | **The hub relays HLS as MPEG-TS to a player that asked for a continuous stream.** A link ends in `.ts` unless the channel's address ends in `.m3u8`. When the source answers a `.ts` link with an HLS playlist, the hub follows the playlist as an HLS player would, from three segments before the live edge, fetches each new segment once, decrypts AES-128, and sends the segments one after another as one MPEG-TS stream. From a master playlist it takes the variant with the highest bandwidth, up to 1080 lines, that carries its own sound. A stream it cannot relay (fMP4 segments, byte ranges, SAMPLE-AES or DRM, sound only in separate renditions) is passed on as a rewritten playlist, as before. | A channel's address does not always say that it is HLS. The MAG box plays a `.ts` link with `ffrt`, which played the segments listed, about half a minute, and stopped, and the page started it again, over and over. The relay keeps the box on the player that it handles best without guessing the format from the address, and Stalker clients that go by the address get the same. |
| D43 | 2026-11 | **The hub leaves out what a source sends again.** Some sources, when their stream restarts on their side, send their last seconds again with the clock set back. A player that keeps track of what it has played skips them; one that does not, such as the MAG box's, plays them again, and the picture goes back in time. On every continuous stream that it sends, copied or relayed, the hub follows the clock of the first video stream (or the first audio stream, if there is no video) and remembers the pictures that it has passed on by their first 2 KiB. When the clock jumps back more than a second to a picture passed on before, the same in those bytes, it leaves out each stream's packets until that stream passes the point it had reached, and the repeated stretch's tables with them. Continuity counters are made to run on over what it leaves out and over a packet cut short. New pictures with an old clock pass on as they are; re-timing them would be stream repair (M2). | A repeat can be told from new pictures exactly, so leaving it out loses nothing that the player has not had, and every player is spared the replay, whether or not it can skip repeats itself. The log says each time it happens. |

| D51 | 2026-11 | **Each source's guide is read into the database, a generation at a time.** An Xtream source's guide is its server's `xmltv.php`, read with the source's login; a playlist's is the XMLTV guide that it names in `url-tvg` or `x-tvg-url`. A read writes a new generation of the source's programmes a batch at a time, then makes it the current one in one step, and a failed read keeps the old one.
