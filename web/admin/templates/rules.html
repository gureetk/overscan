The channels it acts on

It acts on the channels that match everything filled in. In a pattern, * stands for any characters and ? for one, so Sport* matches names that start with Sport; case does not count. Between slashes, such as /\+1$/ for names that end in +1, a pattern is a regular expression, which can match anywhere.

A new name replaces the whole name. Replace text changes part of the name, as the rules before it left it; in what replaces a regular expression, $1 is its first group.

A group of that name, made if the lineup has none.

The channels it matches are numbered in a row from this number, in their order, and keep their numbers as others come. Numbers you pinned yourself are skipped, and channels that held the others get new numbers after the highest.

Devices load the logo from this address, so it must be one that they can reach.

The channel is one of the lineup's, by the name that its source gives it, from the same source if it can be. A channel that shows another's programmes an hour late, such as Harbour News +1, takes the guide of Harbour News with Its name, changed: replace +1 with nothing. Another rule then moves its times.

The programmes that the channels show in the guide move by this much: 60 minutes later for a channel that shows them an hour late.

Channels that a source lists more than once, in another quality or as a second feed, become one. Of the channels it acts on, those with the same guide ID, or the same name once words such as HD, FHD and 4K are left out, as you choose, are copies of one channel: the first that devices see shows, from the sources put first if you choose some, then in the quality chosen, and the others are hidden and play in its place, in turn, when its stream cannot start, after the backups you pick yourself. Copies that sources add later join by themselves.
