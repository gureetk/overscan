Transcoding

Which live channels this server makes anew, and how. Overscan sends a channel as its source sends it, unless a device's or account's profile says otherwise, or live TV, a channel's group or the channel itself says to transcode it always or never. The narrowest of them wins: a channel's over its group's, and a group's over live TV's. A channel set never to be transcoded goes as its source sends it to every device; one set always reaches devices that would get it as it is with the profile chosen, while a device whose own profile transcodes keeps its own.

Lower-case letters, digits and hyphens. Accounts, devices, groups and channels name the profile by it.

H.264 plays nearly everywhere. HEVC takes about half the bit rate for the same picture, and far more of the processor to make.

A larger picture is made smaller to fit; a smaller one is never made larger.

A steady picture quality; the bit rate rises and falls with what is on the screen.

For a device on a slow network: the video's bit rate stays below it, and its quality drops where it must.

As the source sends them makes a picture of each half of an interlaced picture, so 50 a second from a 25 a second channel; at most 30 makes one of each, and halves a faster rate, which takes about half the processor.

AAC and Dolby Digital take the first sound track; as the source sends it keeps every track, for a device that plays them.

Players on most devices show interlaced pictures with combed edges, and HDR on an SDR screen washed out. Graphics that map HDR10 to SDR, as the Graphics card says, make a live HDR10 picture SDR once its channel has shown it is HDR10; otherwise, and in software, a live HDR picture keeps its dynamic range.
